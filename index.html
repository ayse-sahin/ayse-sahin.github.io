Questions

What I'm curious about

From behavior, to the brain, to technology.

  1. 1

    How do hidden internal states shape social behavior?

    Much of my early research looked at how people respond to threat, from threatened masculinity to disease avoidance. I'm still drawn to how states we can't directly observe, like threat, disgust or exclusion, steer what people think and do toward others.

  2. 2

    Can we see those states in the brain?

    Questionnaires only capture what people are willing and able to tell us. I want to know what EEG, which is cheap and portable but noisy, can reveal about states like threat, attention or emotion, and where its limits are.

  3. 3

    Could technology respond to them?

    Most brain–computer interfaces translate deliberate commands, like imagining a movement. I'm curious about systems that could instead pick up on internal states such as attention or stress, and what it would take to make them reliable.

About

Between mind
& brain

I studied psychology in Türkiye and spent much of my degree working in experimental labs, with a research traineeship in Barcelona and an exchange semester in the Netherlands along the way.

I’m now doing an MSc in Cognitive Science at the University of Potsdam and work as a research assistant in Prof. Marianne Maertens’s Computational Psychology Lab at TU Berlin, on psychophysics and computational models of visual perception.

Over time, my interests have moved from social psychology toward the brain and neurotechnology, especially EEG and brain–computer interfaces. What has stayed the same is my curiosity about the states people can’t directly see. I’m using this phase to turn my questions into a research project worth committing to.
